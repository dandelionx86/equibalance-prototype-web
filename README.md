EquiBalance: A Responsive Web Application for Equine Nutrition Support
============================================================================

**Project Overview**
EquiBalance is a web app that helps horse owners make nutrition decisions focused on forage, highlighting hay and pasture as the main sources of calories instead of commercial feeds. EquiBalance is unique because it uses horse-specific data, detailed forage analysis, and local supplier information to check hay and pasture quality and spot nutritional gaps. Its algorithm gives personalized advice on supplements, ration balancers, or commercial feeds to ensure each horse gets balanced nutrition. 
Users can enter data in a structured way, access a full feed and supplement database with nutritional details, use a strong calculation tool, and get AI-supported help through an interactive chat. The simple interface works on desktop, tablet, and mobile, so users can get nutrition advice wherever they have internet access.

**Purpose of the Application**
EquiBalance makes it easier to find good local forage, lets users compare the nutrition in feeds and supplements, and helps identify each horse’s dietary needs. The app supports horse owners in providing great nutrition, keeping horses’ digestion healthy, and meeting their specific nutrient needs.

**Project Goals**
EquiBalance aims to:
•	Determine equine nutrition needs more efficiently 
•	Reduce over-reliance on commercial concentrates
•	Optimize equine nutrition
•	Support forage-first feeding programs
 
**Motivation for Development**
Feeding horses with a focus on forage needs a different approach. Some owners can get hay or pasture analysis but do not know how to use the results. Others have to make important choices without testing and need a good way to estimate forage nutrition.
As a horse owner without formal nutrition training, I have faced challenges choosing commercial feeds and supplements, finding reliable hay suppliers, and understanding pasture analysis. I am committed to a forage-first program for my horses, but creating a balanced diet for each one takes a lot of time and effort.
There is a real need to make forage-first equine nutrition information easier to understand and use. A precise nutrition app is important. EquiBalance answers this need by offering a user-friendly, locally connected tool made for forage-first programs.

**Relationship to Existing Systems**
EquiBalance builds on current equine nutrition calculators and management systems, but puts more focus on being easy to use, supporting forage-first nutrition, and connecting to local resources. The app stands out because of its AI-supported chat, which turns results into clear, useful advice for users. By mixing detailed nutrition analysis with easy-to-understand explanations, EquiBalance offers a consistent and user-friendly nutrition solution.

**Significance of the Project**
Feeding horses is both an art and a science, but many owners do not have expert help or good tools to check diets (Dunnett, 2013). This often leads to guesswork, old habits, and health problems like laminitis, colic, or obesity. Nutrition values change with the seasons, forage supply, and new feed formulas. Kentucky Equine Research (2024) says horse owners often overfeed calories, starch, protein, and micronutrients because they overestimate what their horses need. Without easy-to-use tools, these mistakes happen more. EquiBalance gives evidence-based nutrition info, helping owners save time and avoid over- or underfeeding. Checking horse diets regularly is important as work, health, and seasons change. EquiBalance provides up-to-date nutrition data for accurate adjustments.

**Conclusion**
EquiBalance shows how a user-focused design can help horse owners and their horses. By bringing all the needed data and clear guidance into one platform, EquiBalance turns a confusing and slow process into a simple, efficient system. It makes equine health and management easier by breaking down complex data and connecting science with horse owners, offering a solution that shapes the future of forage-first feeding.
